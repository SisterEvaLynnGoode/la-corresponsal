El taller de carteles — Teacher Guide

Propaganda unit · ten meetings · student packet is 16 pages

Students take apart eight real posters, name ten techniques, then design a poster for a cause they choose. The unit ends with the counter-poster: the slogan their opponents would print, using a technique they just learned. Two of the eight posters are still in copyright and live outside the published site — see the note at the bottom of page 2.

Printing, and the two copyrighted posters

Six posters are public domain and print from anywhere. The UFW strike poster (page 3) and Sister Boom Boom (page 11) are still in copyright, so their image files stay off the public site: they print from your machine and show a labelled box if you print the packet from the website. Print student copies from the project folder, or project those two pages in class.

The AI poster

Page 7 is a poster generated this year that claims to be from a 1936 "American-Soviet Commonwealth" — a country that never existed. Students look for the tells: letters that dissolve under a close look, flags of nothing, no printer or union mark in any corner, and no archive it came from. The honest ending to that discussion is that it still works on the eye: the technique does its job even when nobody made the thing on purpose.

Teaching notes

Teaching notes

Why labour and not race

The two pairs are chosen so that the heat stays on who paid the printer. Day 1 is a company poster and a union poster aimed at the same workers, asking opposite things. Day 2 is the American and Soviet recruiting posters: the same pointing finger, countries that spent a century as enemies, and no way to tell from the technique which one was right. Day 3 does it a third time with an advertisement and a public-health poster.

The question that runs the unit

«¿Quién pagó este cartel, y qué quiere que yo haga?» Ask it every day, about every poster, including the one on the student's own desk on day 9.

What to watch for

  • Posters that are flyers. Four sentences and a border. The three-second test on day 8 fixes this better than you can by telling them.
  • A cause with no action. "Stop racism" is a belief, not a call. Push for what a reader does this week: sign, ask, show up, turn it off, bring one.
  • Students who can't draw. One symbol, big, is the whole requirement. Lettering counts as art.
  • The counter-poster going soft. If a student writes a weak slogan for the other side, they have dodged the point. Make them write the one that would actually hurt.

Grading

Fifteen points, the same table the student uses on page 16. Spelling is not on it — accents on the slogan are coached on page 10 as the shop's house style.
